# Study 001 — "Because Attention Moves" — Creative Brief

First client-facing Instagram Reel. Original prompt: *"a video that says why do we need motion editors?"* — refined below. This brief doubles as the worked example for the `big-video-project` skill; produced from a style analysis of the reference video rather than a full interview (the open questions are listed at the end).

## The reference, decoded

Reference: yuk.aji — *LILIUM — Experimental Motion Study* (Instagram Reel, ~15s, 9:16). Its style system:

1. **One subject treated as a specimen.** A single red lily, dissected like a botanical plate — close-ups labeled *Bud, Tepal, Stigma, Stamen, Receptacle*.
2. **Design-canvas language.** Selection handles, dashed guides, annotation labels, bezier scribbles — the viewer is watching a designer's screen, not a finished film. This is the signature move.
3. **Strict palette.** Signal red + cream/off-white + black, with the yellow of the stamens as the only accent.
4. **Oversized kinetic type** that bleeds past the frame edges (*IN BLOOM*, *LIVING GEOMETRY*, *STUDIES*) and interleaves in depth with the imagery — the flower sits in front of the letters.
5. **Alternating framing.** Letterboxed horizontal canvas band inside the vertical frame for "workspace" beats; full-bleed for hero beats.
6. **Texture passes as beats.** A glitch/datamosh grayscale moment; a particle/data overlay moment. One texture per beat, never stacked.
7. **Editorial packaging.** Title reads like a journal cover ("LILIUM — Experimental Motion Study"), implying a series.

What to take: the grammar — specimen + annotation + oversized interleaved type + one-texture-per-beat + editorial numbering.
What to change: the vocabulary — different palette, and the specimen is **motion itself**, not a flower. That is the line between homage and copy.

## Purpose (clarified)

This is a **debut of craft**: the video's job is to show the work and announce *"what I do is here."* It is not an ad aimed at a client's pain point. The question "why do we need motion editors?" stays — it is the door into the craft — but its job changes: it is the **title of a study**, answered by demonstration, landing on identity. Never a lecture.

## The idea (one sentence)

Dissect motion the way LILIUM dissected a lily: put a dead-static frame on the table, label the anatomy that brings it to life (timing, spacing, easing, weight, rhythm), show the editor's hand doing the work on screen, and let the demonstration answer the title question — then sign it.

## Audience & placement

- Instagram Reels, 9:16, ~18 seconds, designed sound-off (type carries everything; sound rewards those who have it on).
- Audience: potential clients — founders, brands, creators — meeting this account for the first time. The video introduces the craft and the person; it should leave them knowing what a motion editor does and that one just arrived.
- Desired action: follow + profile visit; DM/inquiry is the bonus. CTA stays soft on video, direct in caption.

## Style DNA

- Palette: pick ONE signal color that is yours (not yuk.aji's red) + off-white + black. Same discipline, different flag.
- Type: oversized grotesk or editorial serif caps, bleeding off-frame, depth-interleaved with imagery.
- Mix: ~90% motion graphics / screen-canvas language, 10% found or shot footage inside the "specimen" frames.
- Textures: one glitch pass (beat 4 only), one annotation/canvas system (beats 1, 3). Never stacked.

- Non-goals: not a showreel, not a tutorial, not an argument thread. One idea, proven once, in under 20 seconds.

## Issues with the original idea

1. ~~Wrong question for the audience.~~ **Resolved by clarification.** The purpose is a debut of craft ("show my work; what I do is here"), not a client-pain ad — so the question stays, recast as the *title of a study* answered by demonstration. The client-problem framing ("why does no one stop on my posts") was rejected too: it makes the video about the viewer's failure, when this video is about the craft arriving.
2. **A question invites a lecture, and a 15-second lecture loses.** Telling people motion matters is weaker than showing a still frame die and the same frame live. The video must *be* the proof — it's also your portfolio piece. (Still holds under the new framing: the question is asked once, then only demonstrated.)
3. **Defensive debut.** A first post that justifies the profession can read as insecurity. Framing it as "Study 001" positions you as an authority already mid-body-of-work, not someone asking permission to exist. *(Confirmed by user.)*
4. **Style borrowed too literally.** The reference artist is recognizable, and your target audience overlaps with people who will recognize the system (red field, handles, botanical dissection). Take the grammar, swap the vocabulary: your palette, and motion itself as the specimen. Credit the inspiration if the resemblance stays visible. *(Confirmed by user.)*
5. **No CTA or positioning in the vague idea.** A potential client needs to leave knowing what you do and what to do next. End card + caption strategy above fixes this. Caption first line under the debut framing: "Someone has to decide how things move." (mirrors the answer line; the old client-pain caption is retired with issue 1).
6. **One video is not a presence.** A single post proves nothing about consistency — the thing clients actually buy. The numbered series makes the format compound: Study 002 EASING, 003 TIMING, 004 WEIGHT… each one a pitch and a portfolio piece.
7. **Instagram mechanics were unaddressed.** Design sound-off (all meaning in type), the first frame is the cover (make "STILL." read as intentional, not broken — the visible selection handles and cursor do that), and keep labels out of the right-side UI rail and bottom caption zone. The reference's letterboxed band is not just style — it is safe-zone management. Keep it.
8. **Scope trap.** Six beats including five micro-loops is a serious After Effects build. If hours are tight, ship the 4-beat cut (drop beat 4, fold before/after into beat 5) rather than stall. Imperfect and posted beats perfect and pending.
9. **The title "motion editor."** It's not the searched term — "motion designer" / "motion graphics" are. Either own the unusual title as a differentiator (define it on screen: "motion editor — the person who decides how things move") or bridge it in the caption/hashtags with the standard terms. Decide; don't drift.

## Sound — LOCKED (revised after v6): no music; the film carries its own score

**teti's revision: "I don't like any music."** No licensed track, in Instagram or anywhere.
The soundtrack is synthesised in `sound.py` — a beat and a sound for every motion — and burnt
into the export. What survives from the earlier plan is its idea, not its record: *a bend is
an ease made audible*. The bend is now a synth glide whose pitch follows the exact easing the
cursor drags (09.32→09.96), so the sync is not an alignment — it is the same curve. The beat
runs at 75 BPM from frame 0, which makes the film exactly six bars; the cuts land a pushed
sixteenth after the quarter, consistently, which is what turns them into a groove. Nothing is
licensed, so the same file plays on a portfolio site or in a client send.

### The earlier plan, kept as the record of why (retired)

"Parisienne Walkways" (Gary Moore) — the track with the famous high sustained bend. Chosen by
teti at the time; the concept fusion was real:

**Why it works conceptually.** A guitar bend is an ease made audible — a pitch traveling along a curve instead of jumping. The most famous sustained bend in guitar history, under a video whose thesis is "someone has to decide how things move," is not background music; it's the argument in a second medium. Sync plan (structural, not timestamped — exact times depend on the version/section chosen):

- Moody intro phrase → **STILL.** + study title. Long holds, almost no cutting.
- The moment the note **bends up** → the cursor drags the easing curve and the frozen frame comes alive. Motion begins in sound and image on the same frame.
- The **sustain** (the held scream) → the hero line holds on screen, letters slowly interleaving with the imagery. Don't cut during the sustain — hold with it. Restraint here is itself a craft flex.
- The phrase resolve → end card.

**What it changes.** The 120 BPM click-grid plan is dead — this is a slow blues ballad (~60 BPM feel, roughly one beat per second). Cut to *phrases*, not a grid: fewer, longer, more confident shots. The video's temperament shifts from clinical/techy to soulful — which is also more distinctive; every motion-design reel uses glitchy electronic, almost none use a 1979 blues ballad. Runtime may want to stretch to ~20–22s so the phrases breathe; that's fine for Reels. The glitch texture pass (beat 4) should soften or go — datamosh fights this mood.

**Version — LOCKED: studio (1979 original).** Consequences:
- The bend/sustain is short (~1–2s), so the payoff hold is tight, not luxurious: hero line lands ON the bend-up, holds through the sustain, cuts on its release. Punchy beats languid — runtime comes back to ~18–20s.
- Clean recording, no crowd noise — matches the clean specimen aesthetic. No decision needed there.
- Window: pick the ~20s around the main solo bend (the guitar phrase everyone recognizes), not the vocal verse — the edit needs the instrumental emotion, and the type carries the words.

**Practical checks (do these before building the cut):**
1. Check it's available in **Instagram's audio library on your account** (availability varies by region and account type — Creator accounts get most library music; Business accounts are restricted to royalty-free). If it's there, using it through IG's library is licensed and helps reach.
2. Off Instagram (portfolio site, Vimeo, client sends), the track is NOT licensed — keep an alternate scored version (a moody slow-blues royalty-free cue from Artlist/Epidemic) for anywhere outside IG.
3. If it's not in your library: a high-sustain blues-guitar soundalike cue is the fallback that keeps the concept intact.
